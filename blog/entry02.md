# Entry 2: Crime Scene Investigation
<h1>1. Content / What I Found</h1>
<p>Since my last blog entry, I’ve been researching crime scene investigation and learning more about the technology that forensic investigators use. Before doing this research, I mostly knew about fingerprints and DNA, but I didn’t realize how many different tools and technologies are involved in investigating a crime scene.</p>

<p>One technology I found interesting is an alternate light source (ALS). ALS is a special type of light that helps investigators see certain evidence that might be difficult to notice with normal lighting. Some materials react to the light and become easier to see. I thought this was interesting because it shows how investigators can find evidence that isn’t always visible right away.</p>

<p>Another technology I researched is 3D laser scanning. Instead of only using regular photographs or drawings, investigators can use laser scanners to create a detailed 3D model of a crime scene. This allows them to look at the scene again and understand where different pieces of evidence were located.</p>

<p>I also learned about automated fingerprint searching, which helps investigators compare fingerprints found at crime scenes with fingerprint records. The FBI uses fingerprint technology to help identify possible matches and investigate cases.</p>

<p>I found these technologies interesting because I didn’t know how advanced crime scene investigations could be. I used to think investigators mostly relied on collecting fingerprints and DNA, but now I understand that technology plays a bigger role.</p>

<h1>2. Sources</h1>
<p>I started by looking at websites that provide reliable information about forensic science. I used the National Institute of Justice (NIJ) because it has research and articles about forensic technology. I also used the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) to learn about how forensic scientists use fingerprints, DNA, and other scientific methods.</p>

<p>Some of the specific articles I found helpful were:</p>

<p>Landscape Study of Alternate Light Sources – This helped me understand how special lighting can help investigators find evidence.
Crime Scene Documentation: Weighing the Merits of Three-Dimensional Laser Scanning – This explained how 3D scanning can be used to document crime scenes.</p>
<p>Science and Technology – This helped me learn about the FBI’s forensic work and how fingerprint and DNA analysis are used.</p>
<p>At first, I was looking up general information about forensics, but I realized I needed to be more specific. I started searching for actual technologies used by investigators instead of only looking at what forensic science means. This helped me find more useful information for my topic.</p>
